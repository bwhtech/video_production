## What works (steal)

1. **Proxy learner.** Hussain asks the questions the audience has ("why does my bank SMS say credited?"). In animation: a curious learner character who asks + guesses wrong.
2. **Fear-first hook.** Name the fear ("debit/credit is not my cup of tea") and promise to kill it.
3. **Entity concept early.** The business is a separate person from its owner.
4. **"Categorise first, don't think debit/credit yet."** Separating the steps is the real unlock.
5. **Misconception moments** — bank SMS "credited"; "buying stock is an expense" (no — asset until sold); "loan given vs loan taken".
6. **One running business** through the whole cycle (invest → deposit → buy → pay → sell → collect → profit → close).
7. **Ending on the tally** — "seeing the books balance" is the emotional payoff.
8. **History hook** (counting → Mesopotamia → Pacioli 1494) — short and accurate, as a cold open.

## What to do better

| Problem in the lecture | Our fix |
|---|---|
| "No logic, just mug it up." | **Derive** debit/credit from the accounting equation. Left side of A = L + E is the debit side; right side is the credit side. Expenses shrink equity → debit; income grows equity → credit. Then AED-LIC (or DEAD-CLIC) becomes a *summary* of something understood, not a spell. |
| Statements first, transactions later (top-down, abstract). | Bottom-up: one tiny business, one transaction at a time, watch the equation balance. Statements appear as "snapshots" of what we already built. |
| Double entry + equation shown ~45–50 min in, as asides. | The equation is lesson 2 and the spine of every lesson. |
| Equity filed under "liability" (Indian 4-head convention) with "investor is a liability". | Teach equity as its own bucket ("what the owner has in the business"); then show that Indian charts of accounts often group it under Liabilities — same maths. |
| Jargon dropped live (COGS, accrual, perpetual/periodic, provisional accounts, direct/indirect). | Each term earns its place: concrete story first, word second, one term per scene. Skip perpetual vs periodic, provisional accounts, direct/indirect split. |
| No visuals of balance; spreadsheet + ERP screens. | A physical balance metaphor + buckets that fill/empty, every time. |
| One long session, practice only for the proxy learner. | 12–15 short lessons, each ends in a checkpoint quiz + practice set solved at the start of the next lesson. |
| Golden rules (Real/Personal/Nominal) — what Indian students hear everywhere — never addressed. | One dedicated lesson bridging the Indian golden rules to the equation method, so the viewer can talk to any Indian accountant/CA. |
