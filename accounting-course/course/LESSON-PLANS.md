## Module 2 · Making Money

### L4 · Making Money: Revenue & Expenses  (~5:30)

- **New idea:** revenue increases equity, expenses decrease equity; profit = revenue − expenses, and
  profit belongs to the owner.
- **Builds on:** equation + dual aspect from L3 (solved in "Last time" with Aman A1–A4).
- **Transactions:** T6 (rent ₹5,000 cash), T7 (cash sales ₹18,000).
- **Cold open:** T6 on the scale: cash −5,000, no asset arrives, no debt shrinks. The scale tips…
  and wobbles. What's on the other side?
- **Build beats:**
  1. The Equity jar gets two pockets: *Capital* and *Profit*. The rent comes out of Meera's share —
     `83,000 = 38,000 + 45,000`. That's an **expense**: money used up to run the business.
  2. T7: two weeks of chai (time-lapse Apr 2 → 15: glasses clinking, a counter ticking). **Raju**,
     her new helper, is introduced here (tray + name chip) so his salary in L8 has a face. ₹18,000
     cash in; Meera's share grows → `1,01,000 = 38,000 + 63,000`. That's **revenue**.
  3. **Profit** = revenue − expenses = ₹13,000 so far. It sits in the owner's pocket of equity —
     "profit belongs to the owner" (Umair's year-end insight, made visual).
  4. Expanded equation slides in: `A = L + Capital + Revenue − Expenses`.
  5. **Misconception:** "every rupee that leaves is an expense". Test: *is it used up, gone for
     good?* Rent → yes. Cart (T3) → no, it's still standing there.
- **Worked → faded → solo:** T6 (worked); T7 (faded); solo what-ifs (marked as "imagine", not in the
  books): Meera pays ₹300 for a gas refill — *which two things changed?* [Cash −300, Profit −300 →
  expense] Meera buys a second kettle for ₹1,500? [asset — swap on the left]
- Equity is drawn as Meera's tag unfolding into the **Equity card** with two pockets (Capital | Profit);
  the second pocket is unlabelled until profit is derived. Claims on the right pan are tags, never jars.
- "Last time" card 3 names it: paying back a loan is **not** an expense.
- **Recap:** two pockets of equity; revenue fills, expenses drain; profit = the difference.
- **Your Turn:** (1) Is the ₹36,000 cart an expense? [No] (2) Profit after T6 and T7? [₹13,000]
  (3) Which side of the scale does profit live on? [Right — inside equity]
- **Tease:** An office next door wants chai every day — and will pay at month-end.

### L5 · Profit Is Not Cash  (~6:30) — **Checkpoint 2**

- **New idea:** accrual — revenue counts when earned, expenses when incurred, not when cash moves.
- **Builds on:** revenue/expense from L4.
- **Transactions:** T8 (deposit ₹15,000 in bank), T9 (Infotech tab ₹6,000 on credit), T10 (pay
  Gopal ₹5,000), T11 (₹4,000 catering advance), T12 (Infotech pays ₹4,000 by UPI).
- **Cold open:** Apr 16 — Meera sends Infotech the bill for the first fortnight's tab (Priya's
  "chai for the whole floor, pay later" offer was planted in L4's tease, so the chai has already been
  drunk). Meera's revenue goes up. Her galla doesn't.
- **Build beats:** two needles on the stall's wall — **PROFIT** and **CASH** — and they start to
  disagree.
  1. T8: cash → bank. Profit needle still; cash just changes pockets.
  2. T9: credit sale ₹6,000 — **receivable** (Infotech owes us; an asset). Profit +6,000, cash 0.
     "Earned when the chai is drunk, not when the money arrives." → **accrual**.
  3. T10: pay Gopal ₹5,000. Cash −5,000, profit 0. **Misconception:** paying a supplier isn't an
     expense — it shrinks a liability. Defines **payable** (mirror of receivable).
  4. T11: ₹4,000 advance for May 5 catering. Cash +4,000, profit 0. **Misconception:** an advance
     isn't income yet — it's a liability ("we owe them an event").
  5. T12: Infotech pays ₹4,000 by UPI. Bank +4,000, receivable −4,000. Profit 0. Collecting isn't
     earning — we earned it on T9.
  6. Result: profit ₹19,000; cash in hand ₹35,000; bank ₹19,000 — the needles tell different stories.
     **Cash basis vs accrual basis**, one line each; businesses use accrual because it shows what was
     really earned.
- **Worked → faded → solo:** T9 (worked), T10 (faded), T11 (second faded — Meera guesses "income",
  ✗ stamp; the advance idea is new here so it cannot be a solo), T12 (the only solo, countdown).
- **Recap:** two needles; earned ≠ received; spent ≠ incurred.
- **Your Turn:** (1) T11's advance — revenue in April? [No — liability] (2) Paying Gopal: expense?
  [No — liability down] (3) After T12, how much does Infotech still owe? [₹2,000]
- **Checkpoint 2 Challenge — Aman A5–A9:** effect on profit and on cash. Answers: A5 +9,000 /
  +9,000 · A6 −2,000 / −2,000 · A7 +1,500 / 0 · A8 0 / −2,000 · A9 0 / +1,000. Aman's profit so far:
  ₹8,500.
- **Tease:** Meera's phone buzzes: "Your account is CREDITED with ₹15,000." Great news… right?

## Module 3 · The Secret Language

### L6 · Debit & Credit Are Just Left & Right  (~7:00)

- **New idea:** debit = left, credit = right. Each account's "home side" follows from where it sits
  in the equation. Total debits = total credits, always.
- **Builds on:** equation (L3), revenue/expense as equity (L4), Khata's left/right pages (L1 plant).
- **Transactions:** replay T1, T3, T5, T6, T7, T9; bank SMS uses T8.
- **Cold open:** bank SMS "CREDITED ₹15,000" (T8). Meera beams — credit = good! Khata: "In *your*
  books, that's a debit." Meera's face.
- **Build beats:**
  1. One-line history: Latin *debere* (to owe) / *credere* (to trust) via Pacioli — then: forget the
     meanings. **Debit means left. Credit means right.** Khata opens: L1's payoff.
  2. The equation has a left (Assets) and a right (Liabilities + Equity). Each account lives on its
     home side. **Increase = write it on the home side; decrease = the other side.**
  3. Assets: home = left → increase is a debit. T1 Cash ↑ → Dr; Capital ↑ (right) → Cr.
  4. Liabilities: home = right → T5 Gopal ↑ → Cr; Stock ↑ → Dr.
  5. Revenue grows equity → credit (T7). Expenses: **derived, not decreed** — take L4's
     `A = L + Capital + Revenue − Expenses`, move Expenses across the equals sign:
     `A + Expenses = L + Capital + Revenue`. Expenses live on the left → debit (T6). Drawings get only
     a 3 s `D` tile here ("behave like expenses — we meet them on Apr 30"); the Drawings pocket is
     built in L8.
  6. Summary chip: **DEAD CLIC** — read off the rearranged equation. Visible bridge first:
     `Revenue → Income` ("revenue, or income"). A summary of what you just worked out, not a spell.
  7. "Every transaction: total left = total right." It's the scale in a new costume — side-by-side
     morph of scale → khata.
  8. **Bank SMS myth — two cameras:** Meera's books: Bank (asset) ↑ → Dr. The bank's books: Meera's
     deposit is money the bank *owes* her (liability) ↑ → Cr. "Same money. Opposite books."
- **Worked → faded → solo:** T7 (worked), T9 (faded: Cr side blank), T10 and T11 (solo).
- **Recap:** left/right; home side; DEAD CLIC; the SMS is the bank's view.
- **Your Turn:** (1) T12 — Dr? Cr? [Dr Bank 4,000 / Cr Infotech 4,000] (2) Why is an expense a debit?
  [it shrinks equity, whose home is the right; decreases go on the left] (3) *Imagine* Meera pays a
  bill from the bank and the SMS says "debited ₹8,000". In Meera's books, is Bank debited or credited?
  [Credited] *(hypothetical — T15 hasn't happened yet)*

### L7 · The Golden Rules, Decoded  (~6:30) — **Checkpoint 3**

- **New idea:** the Indian golden rules (Personal / Real / Nominal) are the same entries in another
  dialect.
- **Builds on:** Dr/Cr from L6.
- **Transactions:** T1, T2, T3, T5, T6, T7 run both ways; T10 solo.
- **Cold open:** Meera's CA friend at the stall, rapid-fire: "Debit the receiver, credit the giver!
  Debit what comes in, credit what goes out! Debit all expenses!" Meera's eyes spin.
- **Build beats:**
  1. Heritage hook (20s): bahi-khata, *naam* (debit) and *jama* (credit). "Indian traders have kept
     double-entry books for centuries" (no "first" claim).
  2. Three account families: **Personal** (people & organisations — Ravi Mama, Gopal, Infotech, the
     Bank, and Meera's own Capital/Drawings), **Real** (things — cash, cart, stock), **Nominal**
     (expenses and incomes).
  3. Each rule + its translation (table from `research/accounting-research.md` §2.3):
     Real: comes in = Dr (asset ↑) · Personal: receiver Dr / giver Cr · Nominal: expenses Dr, incomes Cr.
  4. **Split screen:** golden rules left, equation method right, same transaction. Lines slide
     together and *click* — identical entries. Run T2, T3, T5, T6, T7.
  5. Gotchas: bank account = Personal (an organisation), not Real; Capital = Personal (Meera).
  6. Cheat-sheet: Real ≈ assets · Personal ≈ people (receivables, payables, loans, bank, capital,
     drawings, **advances — a customer the stall owes something to**) · Nominal ≈ P&L. The advance
     is named here so Your Turn Q1 is answerable.
  Heritage beat ≤ 12 s; this is the ONE place Khata's name is explained.
- **Worked → faded → solo:** T2 (worked: Real in / Personal giver), T6 (faded), **T12** (solo: Bank
  Personal receiver / Infotech Personal giver — "both Personal!"; T10 was L6's solo).
- **Your Turn:** (1) T11 the advance — golden-rule tags? [Cash: Real, comes in → Dr; Advance:
  Personal (representative), giver → Cr] (2) Is the Bank Real or Personal? [Personal]
  (3) Which family is Rent? [Nominal]
- **Checkpoint 3 Challenge — Aman A2, A3, A5, A6, A7, A9:** Dr/Cr + golden-rule tag. Answers:
  A2 Dr Cash (Real in) / Cr Bank loan (Personal giver) · A3 Dr Cart (Real in) / Cr Cash (Real out) ·
  A5 Dr Cash (Real in) / Cr Sales (Nominal income) · A6 Dr Stall rent (Nominal expense) / Cr Cash
  (Real out) · A7 Dr School canteen (Personal receiver) / Cr Sales (Nominal income) · A9 Dr Cash
  (Real in) / Cr Advance from customer (Personal giver).

## Module 4 · Keeping the Books

### L8 · The Journal  (~5:30)

- **New idea:** the journal (day-book, *rojmel*) — every transaction written in date order:
  date, debit line, "To" credit line, amounts, narration. Compound entries.
- **Builds on:** Dr/Cr + golden rules (L6–L7).
- **Transactions:** T13 (cash sales ₹22,000), T14 (Infotech ₹4,000 on credit), T15 (salary ₹8,000 by
  UPI), T16 (₹3,300 to Ravi Mama = ₹3,000 loan + ₹300 interest), T17 (drawings ₹3,000).
- **Cold open:** Apr 30 rush; Meera scribbles on a tissue; a gust blows it away. "How will she
  remember any of this next April?"
- **Build beats:**
  1. Khata opens to a journal page. Anatomy, one column at a time: Date · Particulars (Dr account
     first; the credit account indented with "To") · Dr ₹ · Cr ₹ · narration in brackets.
  2. T13 written in full (worked), with golden-rule tags appearing on each line (bible device 5).
  3. T14 (interleaved repeat of T9).
  4. T15 — paid from the bank (faded: Meera writes the credit line).
  5. **T16 compound entry:** one payment, two debits — Loan 3,000 + Interest 300 / Cash 3,300.
     **Misconception:** "repaying a loan is an expense" → ✗ only the ₹300 interest is.
  6. T17 drawings — **misconception:** drawings aren't an expense; they reduce equity, never touch

     stays ₹50,000; Profit untouched. Journal anatomy introduces `A/c` with a chip; every line gets an

- **Worked → faded → solo:** T13 / T15 / T17.
- **Your Turn:** (1) Why isn't all of T16's ₹3,300 an expense? [₹3,000 just repays the loan]

### L10 · Month-End Surprises  (~7:00)

- **New idea:** adjustments — expenses that belong to April even without cash moving: unpaid bills,

- **Builds on:** accrual (L5), journal + ledger (L8–L9).
- **Transactions:** T18 (electricity ₹1,000 unpaid), T19 (₹10,000 stock used), T20 (depreciation ₹1,000).

- **Build beats:**
  1. T18: April's electricity bill arrives on Apr 30, due in May. It's April's cost → Dr Electricity

  2. T19: stock jar — ₹14,000 went in (T4 + T5), a count finds ₹4,000 left → ₹10,000 used → Dr Cost of
     supplies used 10,000 / Cr Stock 10,000. Callback to Umair's moment: stock is an asset until used.
  3. T20: the cart helps for ~3 years. ₹36,000 ÷ 36 months = ₹1,000 a month → Dr Depreciation 1,000 /

## Module 5 · Reading the Story

### L12 · The Profit & Loss Statement  (~5:30)

- **New idea:** build and read the P&L (the movie of April).
- **Builds on:** TB (L11); movie metaphor (L1).
- **Cold open:** L1's opening shot replayed — night, galla, "Did Meera make money?" This time Khata
  opens.
- **Build beats:**
  1. Income and expense lines leave the TB and line up as a film strip, top to bottom:
     Sales 50,000 (cash 40,000 + credit 10,000) − Cost of supplies used 10,000 = **Gross profit
     40,000**; − Rent 5,000 − Salary 8,000 − Electricity 1,000 − Depreciation 1,000 − Interest 300 =
     **Net profit ₹24,700**.
  2. Read it like a story: gross profit = what's left after paying for the chai itself; net profit =
     after running the stall. "For every ₹100 of chai, Meera keeps about ₹49."
