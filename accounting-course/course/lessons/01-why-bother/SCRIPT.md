## Line 3 — Accounting is counting (Scene 2)

**Time:** 0:33 – 1:05
**Delivery:** Storyteller. Quick, light, a little wonder.

    Accounting sounds scary. But it started with something you already know how to do — counting.
    Five thousand years ago, in Mesopotamia, farmers pressed little marks into clay to count their sheep and grain.
    About five hundred years ago, in Venice, a monk named Luca Pacioli wrote down a clever system for keeping track of money. And Indian traders have kept the same kind of books — the bahi-khata — for centuries.
    Today the books live inside apps. But the idea hasn't changed.

## Line 5 — Two questions (Scene 4)

**Time:** 1:20 – 2:10
**Delivery:** Clear, confident. Slow down on the two questions.

    Every business owner — a chai stall, a startup, a giant company — really wants the answers to just two questions.
    One: Am I making money?
    Two: What do I own — and what do I owe?
    Accounting answers both, with two reports.
    The first is called the **Profit and Loss statement**. Think of it as a movie. It plays the story of a period — a day, a month, a year — and tells you whether you earned more than you spent.
    The second is the **Balance Sheet**. That's a photo. One moment, frozen. Everything you own, and everything you owe, right now.
    A movie, and a photo. Profit and Loss, and Balance Sheet. We'll build both for Meera, by the end of April.

## Line 6 — The business is a separate person (Scene 5)

**Time:** 2:10 – 3:15
**Delivery:** This is the big idea. Patient. Let the pauses breathe.

    But first, the most important idea in this whole lesson.
    Meera and Meera's Chai are not the same.
    [PAUSE 1s]
    To an accountant, the stall is its own little person. It has its own money, its own things, its own debts.
    So when Meera takes fifty thousand rupees out of her savings and puts it in the galla, the money now belongs to the stall. And the stall remembers that it came from Meera.
    Here's where most new founders slip. "It's my stall, so its money is my money."
    [SFX: stamp]
    Nope. If Meera pays her home electricity bill from the galla, that's not a stall expense. And if she buys sugar for the stall with her own phone, the stall still needs to know about it.
    Two people. Two sets of money. This is called the **business entity** idea, and every rule you'll learn sits on top of it.
